"Wow, this is a real dopamine rush! Hey, grab your cup quick, cup!"

Inside-seat at third base, Jamsil Baseball Stadium. Minjae, a five-year office worker and a die-hard fan of the Zeus Kingz for ten years, was already halfway hoarse. His uniform was soaked in sweat from jumping endlessly to the cheering songs, and the beer cup in one hand sloshed with excitement.

Today’s game was truly an unscripted drama. Up until the top of the eighth inning, the Kingz were down 4 to 1, but they steadily clawed back during the bottom of the eighth and ninth innings, finally creating a comic-book-like opportunity with two outs and bases loaded in the bottom of the ninth.

"Those Neo Titans, never fail to disappoint, right? They light the fire just at the perfect moment!"
"Hey, but who’s the pitcher coming up? Kang Hyun-woo?"
"Kang Hyun-woo? Wow, what a blast from the past. He’s the guy who used to throw 155 km/h, right? Now he’s just a B-level bullpen pitcher throwing lame balls. Today, Taeseong is gonna give him a proper retirement with a homer!"

Minjae and the surrounding Kingz fans were already in a festive mood. What kind of team are the Neo Titans? They are the most perennial last-place team in KBO history and the symbol of bullpen collapses that crumble even when victory is near. Minjae belted out the cheering song for the Kingz' cleanup hitter, Choi Taeseong, and fixed his gaze on the scoreboard.

Into the middle of the roaring ballpark, Kang Hyun-woo was walking onto the mound.

The scoreboard doesn't lie.

[141 km/h]

From the Kingz’ stands came a mix of “Woo!” jeers and mocking cheers, while the Neo section let out deep sighs. Sometimes that very breath was like a sharp blade flying onto the mound where the man stood with two outs and bases loaded in the bottom of the ninth.

Kang Hyun-woo roughly brushed the seams of the baseball gripped in his right hand. The leather’s texture felt especially cold at his fingertips. Just six years ago, when he threw from this mound, the scoreboard would boldly flash 154 or 155. The blazing fastballs that dug into the catcher’s mitt as if tearing it apart were no longer with Hyun-woo.

Three surgical scars crossed his shoulder.

That was the price that devoured Hyun-woo's prime.

"Hoo..."

Exhaling deeply, he saw catcher Juhyung's sign board, masked and waiting. A low outside two-seam fastball.

Hyun-woo nodded lightly. He was no longer the fireballer who thrilled crowds with strikeouts. He was a seasoned veteran with calloused control, gently steering the bat’s center aside to induce grounders. To be frank, he was just the shaky B-level closer of the lower-tier Seoul Neo Titans.

At the plate was Choi Taeseong, the Kingz’ cleanup hitter aiming for the home run crown this year, lightly spinning his bat.

Score: 4 to 3 – a one-run lead. Bases loaded.

One hit here, and the game would be reversed and ended.

"Taeseong hyung! One swing ends it! Let’s make those Neo guys cry!"

Minjae screamed, gripping the fence. Tens of thousands of home fans unleashed overwhelming pressure upon the mound.

Windup.

But Hyun-woo’s eyes were calm. He lifted his left leg high. Though the dynamic form of his prime was gone, instead he lowered his center of gravity to find the most stable release point with a smooth motion. The ball left his fingertips, flying toward the home plate.

Choi Taeseong’s bat swung sharply.

Minjae instinctively raised his arms, ready to celebrate the walk-off home run.

But at that moment, right in front of home plate, the ball curved like magic, dropping low and outside the right-handed batter’s zone. A 140 km/h trajectory, serpent-like in its curve.

Thud!

A dull crack echoed. Minjae’s face hardened. Instead of stretching to the fence, the ball bounced twice on the infield dirt, rolling straight to the shortstop.

The shortstop calmly fielded the ball, tossed it to the second baseman, and then to first base: a smooth double play.

"Out! Game over!"

"No way! To induce a grounder right there!"

Minjae covered his head, sinking to his seat. The surrounding Kingz fans all sighed in frustration. In that bitter moment of having the victory in hand and losing it, Minjae glared at Kang Hyun-woo calmly hugging his catcher on the mound. He thought his prime was gone, but that seasoned game sense and control were too good for such a low-ranked team.

"Hyung, you killed it. How do you sneak that pitch in that spot?"
"Dude, it’s all luck. Taeseong hyung was just a bit late on timing."

Hyun-woo laughed softly, gently tapping Juhyung’s cap. If he were twenty-four, he’d be frustrated at not striking out the batter or tense over the close win. But at thirty-one, Hyun-woo understood—a weary yet warm ease that only those on the verge of breaking could know.

After high-fiving teammates and heading to the locker room, cheers erupted all around, but inside Hyun-woo felt a chill. He knew better than anyone that his fastball’s power was weaker than last week, barely holding on today.

After showering and changing, Hyun-woo turned on his smartphone. Among the overflowing congratulatory messages, one name stood out.

[Yeonju: Great game today! Certified the Titans’ win. Almost done with work, wanna meet at that spot in Cheongdam-dong?]

A faint smile touched Hyun-woo’s lips, then was replaced by bitterness.

Han Yeonju. His seven-year girlfriend and CEO of NextLab, the hottest edu-tech startup in Korea.

Her profile picture was a neat portrait in a business magazine from a week ago: sophisticated, confident, radiant. Meanwhile, Hyun-woo was a baseball player nearing the end of his shelf life, wrapping ice packs on his shoulder every night, uncertain if he’d be cut loose.

It had been different when they first met.

Then, Yeonju was a novice entrepreneur burning the midnight oil in a tiny office, and Hyun-woo was a next-generation ace of KBO earning multi-billion won salaries. He was the one who bought her meals and cheered her on in hard times.

But the world’s clock wasn’t fair. Yeonju’s company skyrocketed with billions in investment while Hyun-woo’s shoulder collapsed. Their places had completely reversed.

[Hyun-woo: Yeonju, you must be tired today. Just head home. I’ll probably be too late if I take the team bus. Let’s meet on the weekend.]

He sent the message and sighed deeply. It was a lie. It was a home game; driving would take just thirty minutes. He just couldn’t face sitting opposite her shining success tonight.

Walking down to the baseball stadium parking lot, Hyun-woo stopped in front of the Neo Titans' GM’s office. Familiar voices drifted through the open door.

"GM, isn’t it premature to discuss trade matchups using Kang Hyun-woo's card? He’s peaked now. Though he barely finished today, the aging curve is clear. Who knows when he might break down."
"But Hyun-woo is a symbolic player for the team; we have to consider fan opinion."
"Symbolism doesn’t pay bills. Some contending teams definitely need veteran bullpen depth. If we want at least a second-round draft pick, we have to move now."

The operations manager’s cold voice stabbed Hyun-woo’s heart.

He smiled bitterly and quietly walked away. He wasn’t angry. The pro world was always like this. Neo Titans couldn’t afford loyalty to a closer throwing 140 km/h fastballs.

Getting into his parked domestic sedan in a corner of the lot, the quiet inside made his shoulder ache fiercely. He swallowed a painkiller and gripped the wheel.

His phone buzzed again. It was Yeonju.

[Yeonju: Liar. What team bus at a home game? I'm already at our usual Cheongdam-dong spot. If you don’t come within 30 minutes, I’m scolding you.]

Hyun-woo rested his forehead on the steering wheel. She was always like that, noticing whenever he tried to avoid her and knocking on the door where he hid. That unchanging kindness, that consistent love, was slowly tightening around his neck.

"Do I really... deserve to be by Yeonju’s side?"

The thought circled in his mind dozens of times a day. Sometimes he wished she would get tired and leave first. But Yeonju was too kind and steadfast not to break first.

Then he thought maybe he should be the one to let go.

Switching to drive, the car left the dark stadium parking lot and flowed into the dazzling Seoul night.

A quiet izakaya in Cheongdam-dong. Opening the door to a private room in the corner, Yeonju looked up from her laptop with a bright smile, wearing glasses and no makeup.

"You’re here! Good job today, our closer."
"You said you’re busy with work, why come all the way here? It’s late."

Hyun-woo pretended to be indifferent as he sat down. Yeonju closed her laptop and gently placed Hyun-woo’s right arm on her lap. She started massaging his elbow and shoulder with a practiced touch.

"You looked like your shoulder was heavy today. Are you okay? Want me to book a doctor? I know a specialist in sports rehab."
"No, I’m okay. It’s pain I face every day anyway."
"Okay my ass. Why do you keep doing this to upset me?"

Yeonju frowned slightly and looked up at Hyun-woo. Meeting her clear eyes, his chest hurt.

"Yeonju."
"Hmm?"
"You said last week your meetings with overseas investors went well. I saw the news."
"Oh, that? The Silicon Valley response was good; I’ll have to go for a business trip next month. Probably gone about a month. I pushed myself to come today to see you often before I leave."

A business trip. To Silicon Valley.

She was now playing on a global scale, while he struggled as a mediocre closer on a neighborhood-like Neo Titans mound.

Hyun-woo sneaked away her hand and put it back in place.

"Don’t massage my shoulder. You’re tired. Take care of yourself."
"Hyun-woo."
"And... maybe we should take some time apart for a while."

The air in the room froze. Yeonju’s touch faltered. Her eyes shook wildly.

"...What’s that all of a sudden?"
"Not sudden. I’ve thought about it a long time. You’re busy and on the rise. But I... you know. This might be my last season. The team’s thinking of trading or cutting me."
"What does that have to do with our relationship!"

Yeonju’s voice rose for the first time, unusually heated.

"It matters. I feel burdened. Standing beside you makes me look so small I can’t stand it."

Hyun-woo pressed down his emotions and spoke with a chilling calm voice. The warm aura forged through hardships unveiled a coldness from the past. It wasn’t a blade aimed at Yeonju but a self-inflicted sharpness.

"Hyun-woo, I’ve never thought that about you. Whether you threw 155 km/h or 140, you were just Kang Hyun-woo to me."
"I appreciate that, but my pride won’t allow it. Sorry. I’ll leave first today."

He stood up. Yeonju reached for his sleeve, but Hyun-woo left without looking back.

Outside, the cold night wind slapped his cheeks. His heart felt hollow yet strangely liberated, as if a long overdue burden was lifted. But soon, a deep loneliness bit into him, and he bit his lips.

Getting into his car and starting the engine, he saw the sports drink and vitamins Yeonju had prepared on the passenger seat—things she never forgot to bring on his game days.

Hyun-woo hit his head against the steering wheel.

"What a fool..."

His shoulders ached. His future was uncertain. The one bright light in his life, the woman, he’d pushed away. For thirty-one-year-old pitcher Kang Hyun-woo, all that remained was the mound he’d face tomorrow and a pitiful 141 km/h fastball.

But Hyun-woo didn’t know yet.

When a person pushed to the edge lets everything go, the baseball gods start reaching out to them in strange ways.

At least, until the urgent call from the club official the next morning.
