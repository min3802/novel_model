Human misfortune often comes without warning, but in the pro world, downfall approaches with a very polite and calm procedure.

9:15 a.m. Kang Hyun-woo was walking down the hallway of the Neo Titans team office located in Seocho-dong. Until a few months ago, when he got off the away bus and came to work, the front office staff would greet him first with bowed heads. But today, one of the assistant managers he met in the hallway flinched when their eyes met and quickly hid in the pantry.

That chilly air said it all.

When he knocked on the general manager’s office door, a heavy voice came from inside.
"Come in."

Song Young-jin, the general manager of the Neo Titans, was rifling through a pile of papers. When Hyun-woo entered, he took off his glasses and placed them on the desk. His face was strangely mixed with remorse and embarrassment. The cold voice of the operations team leader he had overheard outside the office yesterday echoed again in his ears.

"Hyun-woo, are you here? Have a seat."
"Yes, General Manager. You called me."

Hyun-woo quietly put down his bag and sat on the leather sofa across from him. His face, worn by years and injuries, was excessively calm. On the contrary, that warm and stoic expression seemed to weigh even heavier on General Manager Song's mind.

"I'll be direct. A trade was finalized late last night."

The GM slid a document toward Hyun-woo. His gaze fixed on the paper.

[Seoul Neo Titans Pitcher Kang Hyun-woo – Daegu Zeus Kings 2027 Rookie 2nd Round Draft Pick and Cash]

Trade.
It was a transfer to the very team he had just secured a victory against by inducing a ground ball the day before—the Zeus Kings. The Kings were strong contenders for the championship this year, but recently two bullpen pitchers had simultaneously left due to stress fractures, causing an emergency in their relief pitching.

Although his velocity had dropped, they desperately needed a veteran bullpen pitcher who could rely on experience and control in crisis situations.

"The Kings strongly requested you. For our team... you know, it's time to turn toward rebuilding, so we needed draft picks. I'm sorry, Hyun-woo. Sending off one of the founding members like you."

The GM’s words sounded like an excuse. Ultimately, it was the result of calculating to sell a veteran nearing his expiration date at the highest price.

Hyun-woo stared blankly at the paper, then gave a faint bitter smile.
"No, it's professional business; what can be done? The Kings are a championship-level team, so I'm grateful they called a B-level closer like me."

"Hyun-woo, you’re still... the same as ever. In the old days, you’d have broken the office furniture by now."
"I’m older and weaker now; where would I get the strength to break furniture? Besides, I can't afford to pay for damages."

Hyun-woo chuckled and stood up. Maybe because his reaction was so bittersweet, General Manager Song couldn’t meet Hyun-woo’s eyes and instead extended his hand for a shake.

"Go prove your worth. You’re still a usable pitcher."
"Yes. Thank you for everything."

Hyun-woo’s steps were heavy as he left the office. This was the club where he had devoted his twenties, burning a blazing 155 km/h fastball. When he had painfully rehabbed his shoulder and finally returned to the mound, the cheers from the fans were still vivid in his ears. But all those memories were relentlessly settled by the four letters of 'business.'

After descending to the underground parking lot and getting in his car, Hyun-woo sat quietly gripping the steering wheel for a long time.

He checked his smartphone and found no messages from Yeon-ju, whom he had just parted with yesterday. It was a natural outcome. Waiting for contact after those harsh words was selfish.

‘Maybe it’s for the best.’

If he moved down to Daegu, the distance between them would naturally grow. No matter how strong a long-distance relationship is, it tends to become distant, especially when added to their current practical gap, making it easier for Yeon-ju to clear her heart.

A mysterious pain throbbed deep in his chest. It was a scar far more severe than the pain in his operated shoulder.

Before heading to Daegu, Hyun-woo visited a private rehabilitation center near Jamsil Baseball Stadium. The team trainers had to keep the trade secret until it was officially announced, and more than anything, he wanted a cold, honest check of the true state of his failing shoulder.

Dr. Kim, the director of the center and a longtime helper in Hyun-woo’s rehab, sighed deeply while showing the MRI of Hyun-woo’s shoulder on the monitor.

"Hyun-woo, let me be frank. When you throw your two-seam, you force your fingertips to rotate, right?"

"...Can you tell?"

"If I couldn’t, I’d have to quit being a doctor. Since your velocity dropped, you’re using your elbow angle and wrist snap unusually to maintain control. This means your elbow and wrist are forcibly sharing the load that should be on your shoulder rotator cuff."

Dr. Kim tapped on the monitor.

"If you keep pitching like this for two more months, your elbow ligament will give out. Then it’s really retirement. You’re going to the Kings, right? They're different from the Neo Titans. Due to championship pressure, they’ll put you on in tight situations every game. Do you think your body can hold up?"

Hyun-woo buttoned his shirt silently. His right shoulder, reflected in the mirror, was slightly drooped compared to the left.

"I have to endure it. Whether I finish my career after fulfilling the contract or not."

"You’re stubborn. Don’t you have a girlfriend? Did you tell President Han?"

"I didn’t. And she’s someone who doesn’t concern me anymore. I broke up with her yesterday."

Dr. Kim adjusted his gown and stared hard at Hyun-woo.

"Your pride has gotten you into trouble again. What did President Han do to you?"

"Exactly. I stopped because I couldn’t take receiving her excessive feelings; I’m not even human if I take more."

Hyun-woo picked up his bag and stood.

"I’ll get settled in Daegu and contact you. Please prescribe me some extra medicine."

That afternoon, KBO officially announced Kang Hyun-woo’s trade, spreading the news through the baseball world. Neo Titans fans crashed the team website, saying, ‘They traded away the team’s franchise star for a rookie draft pick,’ while the Kings fans protested, ‘Why bring in a 140 km/h batting practice pitcher?’ Nobody welcomed Hyun-woo.

Hyun-woo packed just a few simple bags and loaded them into his car. When he entered the Gyeongbu Expressway at Seoul’s outskirts, the vibrating phone on the passenger seat rang.

The caller ID showed “Restricted Number.”

Usually, such calls were ads or spam, but Hyun-woo pressed the answer button for some unknown reason. Through the car’s Bluetooth speaker came a mechanical, crackling voice.

[Are you player Kang Hyun-woo?]

The middle-aged man’s tone was low and dry, not like a team official or reporter.

"Yes, that’s me. Who is this?"

[I was impressed by your last game with the Seoul Neo Titans. You stopped a bases-loaded rally with a 141 km/h two-seam. Remarkable determination.]

"Just get to the point. I’m driving and busy."

Undeterred by Hyun-woo’s curt reply, the man continued.

[I saw the news about your trade to the Kings. But with that shoulder, you won’t last three months before being released or your pitching career ends. Pitching while chewing up your elbow and wrist won’t last.]

Hyun-woo nearly slammed on the brakes. This man recited the diagnosis he’d just received from Dr. Kim, though he hadn’t told anyone else. Cold sweat beaded down his spine.

"Who are you? Did you investigate me?"

[Not an investigation. I’m making an offer. To a disgraced pitcher who lost his fastball and became a finesse pitcher, I’m giving you a chance to reignite your flame. What if the overwhelming velocity of your past combined with the razor-sharp control you have now?]

It was a crazy claim, like something out of a baseball fantasy novel. A stranger on the other end promising to fix the rotator cuff injury that modern medicine couldn’t.

"This is a prank call. If you contact me like this one more time, I’ll report to the police."

[Kang Hyun-woo, a person who has hit rock bottom needs opportunity more than pride. At midnight tonight, go to the mound at Daegu Kings Park. There you’ll find the clue to the 'flame' you lost.]

Click.

The call ended abruptly. Hyun-woo splashed cold water on his face and threw the phone down irritably. A crazy prank call, surely.

But strangely, the last words made his heart pound. ‘If your past velocity and current control combined.’ The perfect ideal every pitcher dreamed of wouldn’t leave his mind.

The car passed through the Daegu tollgate, the setting sun painting the sky red behind him. It was a city of new beginnings, but Daegu’s nightscape felt unfamiliar and lonely to Hyun-woo.

And as if on cue, at 11:40 p.m., after unpacking and lying on his bed, Hyun-woo found himself dressing and leaving the room. Rationally, it was irrational, but his steps were already heading toward the darkened Daegu Kings Park.
